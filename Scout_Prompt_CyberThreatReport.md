Prompt 1: Cyber Threat Intelligence Report

Input:

Named Entities extracted from the analysis.
DOE CESER guidelines for report structure, citations, bibliography, and analytical language.
ICD-203 standards for analytical estimations.
Requirement to use active voice.

Expected Output:

A structured Cyber Threat Intelligence Report that includes the following sections:
Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF): A concise summary of the most critical information and key findings.
Threat Context: Overview of the threat landscape and relevant background information.
Observed Activities: Detailed account of the activities observed, including tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs).
Potential Impacts: Analysis of the potential consequences if the threat is not mitigated.
Recommended Mitigations: Actionable recommendations to address the identified threat.
The report should adhere to DOE CESER guidelines, follow ICD-203 standards, and use active voice.
All statements must be clear, concise, and factual, avoiding any speculative content.
Properly formatted citations and a bibliography should be included.
If specific references or sources are not provided, the report should state 'source not provided' without inferring information.

Example Output:

**Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF):**
Adversaries have targeted ICS within the energy sector through spearphishing attacks, potentially leading to significant operational disruptions. Immediate actions are necessary to mitigate the risk.

**Threat Context:**
Adversaries have been observed targeting industrial control systems (ICS) within the energy sector. Their primary objective appears to be gaining unauthorized access to control systems to disrupt operations.

**Observed Activities:**
The adversary used a spearphishing email containing a malicious attachment to gain initial access. Upon execution, the malware established communication with a command and control (C2) server, allowing the adversary to issue commands remotely.

**Potential Impacts:**
If left unchecked, the adversary could cause significant disruption to energy production, leading to potential economic losses and safety hazards.

**Recommended Mitigations:**
It is recommended to implement stricter email filtering and user education programs to reduce the risk of spearphishing attacks. Additionally, network segmentation and regular monitoring of outbound traffic should be enforced to detect and block unauthorized communications.
